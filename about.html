About

I make correctness a property, not a hope.

I'm Dayim Shah, a Computer Science graduate from VIT Vellore based in Bengaluru. I work on the data side of software: analytical warehouses, the pipelines that feed them, the APIs that serve them, and the dashboards that make the numbers legible. I like the parts of this work that other people find tedious: idempotency, reconciliation, backfills that don't double-count, tests that describe behaviour instead of pinning a magic number.

My projects tend to start from a failure mode rather than a feature list. Rewind exists because reprocessing historical data by hand quietly ships wrong numbers for weeks. Prism plants deliberate causal structure into synthetic data and then checks whether the analytics layer can rediscover it blind. When the instruments can't, I write down why — knowing where your measurements break is the actual skill.

How I work

  • Correctness before cleverness. A pipeline that's fast and wrong is worse than one that's slow and right. I build the guardrails first.
  • Honest about limits. I document what a system doesn't do and where it would break under load, rather than letting a demo imply more than it proves.
  • Tests that mean something. I prefer invariant and property-based tests over hardcoded expected values, so the suite still protects you when the data changes.
  • End to end. I take projects from schema and ingestion through API, dashboard, Docker, and a live deployment — not just the interesting middle.
